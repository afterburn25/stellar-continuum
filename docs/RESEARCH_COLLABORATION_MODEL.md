# Cross-Polity Joint Research & Scientific Collaboration

Milestone #12 defines how different polities can perform real shared science without turning diplomacy into a `+research%` bonus or merging their technology trees.

## Core rule

**A research agreement creates permission/coordination, not Research Points.**

A joint program progresses only from real contributed:

- Effective Research Labs;
- directed-program capacity;
- specialist facilities;
- expert cohorts/training;
- experimental datasets;
- evidence/samples;
- prototypes/reference hardware;
- research tooling;
- materials/feedstock;
- computational/observatory capacity;
- research contexts and communication links.

A treaty never mints scientific capacity.

## Collaboration types

The research layer recognizes several scientific arrangements:

- **Joint Directed Project** — participants actively co-develop one visible scientific/engineering objective.
- **Shared Observation Program** — participants coordinate observatories/sensors/experiments and exchange selected data.
- **Shared Facility Program** — participants grant real facility/test-environment access.
- **Expert Exchange Program** — participants contribute actual expert cohorts, training, or operating practice.
- **Joint Foreign Technology Study** — participants jointly examine foreign hardware/records/biology while retaining participant-specific Understanding/Operability/Reproduction/Adaptation.

Diplomacy owns how agreements are negotiated, accepted, funded, violated, canceled, or politically interpreted.

## Joint directed programs consume real concurrency

A polity that actively contributes directed research to a Joint Directed Project consumes one of its own available directed-program positions exactly like a domestic major project.

The collaboration cannot bypass the normal progression:

- 1 directed program early;
- 2 after Coordinated Research Networks;
- 4 after Distributed Scientific Portfolios;
- lab-capacity-only after Autonomous Research Portfolios.

A polity that only supplies passive facility/data/sample access does not necessarily consume a directed-program position unless it is actually coordinating active research work.

## Lab scaling is project-wide

Joint labs use the same canonical diminishing-return curve as domestic research.

If Polity A contributes 20 labs and Polity B contributes 12 to one project with 16 recommended labs, the project has **32 assigned labs**, not two independent lab-scaling buckets.

At current seed values:

- first 16 labs count at 1.0 each;
- next 16 count at 0.35 each;
- scaled total = **21.6 lab units**;
- at 100 RP per lab-year = **2160 RP/year before Project Readiness**.

Incorrectly scaling the two flags separately would produce 29.4 lab units and would let diplomatic labels bypass diminishing returns. The benchmark explicitly forbids that.

## Every contribution has a real source

Participant contribution records reference actual state.

Examples:

- labs must exist and be free/eligible;
- facilities must exist, be accessible, and satisfy the real stage capability;
- experts must be real tacit/expert assets;
- samples/data must actually be possessed and shareable;
- materials/feedstock must actually be supplied;
- hardware/tooling must physically exist;
- communications must actually connect participating contexts.

The same asset cannot be counted twice because two participants both reference it.

## Unequal contributions are allowed

Partners need not contribute equally.

One polity may provide most laboratories; another may provide a unique facility; another may provide an alien sample; another may provide expert manufacturing practice.

Scientific contribution and result rights are separate questions.

A participant can contribute a scarce facility yet negotiate limited result rights, or contribute little but receive a broad result package if Diplomacy successfully negotiated that arrangement.

Research enforces what was actually delivered/accessed, not whether the deal was politically wise.

## Contributions without complete theory

A participant does not need complete mastery of the objective to contribute every kind of useful input.

It may contribute:

- observations;
- datasets;
- samples;
- test articles;
- facilities;
- computation;
- materials.

Direct theory/experimental/engineering research contribution requires sufficient objective access, competence, applicability, and authorized capacity for the work being performed.

This supports believable collaborations between technologically asymmetric partners.

## Progress model

For a joint directed project:

1. validate every contribution independently;
2. exclude withdrawn/unavailable/unauthorized/incompatible contributions;
3. aggregate real labs under the canonical diminishing-return curve;
4. verify shared hard facility/evidence/material requirements from real accessible contributions;
5. compute Project Readiness from actual participating competence/facility/evidence/tacit inputs without double-counting;
6. advance one authoritative joint work record;
7. grant competence/practice only to participants that actually performed relevant work;
8. on stage/result transitions, create participant-specific result packages and real delivery records.

Research Pressure remains availability/urgency, not a collaboration speed multiplier.

## Competence from participation

Actual research work can grow relevant theoretical/experimental/engineering competence.

Passive treaty membership, payment, facility ownership without scientific participation, or simple result receipt does not create practical competence.

A participant analyzing shared data can gain appropriate theoretical/experimental experience if it actually performs that work.

## Results are participant-specific

Joint results use the existing technology-exchange package/right model.

Possible result components include:

- scientific summary;
- theory/models;
- datasets;
- engineering blueprints;
- manufacturing process records;
- reference hardware;
- expert/training support;
- operation/manufacture/modification/sharing/resale rights.

Partners can receive different packages.

## Co-development vs passive transfer

A participant can advance its own native node through a joint result only when it truly co-developed the relevant work:

- it actually performed authorized scientific work;
- it used required directed-program capacity when appropriate;
- it had objective access/applicability;
- it receives the necessary result records/rights;
- the joint project reaches the normal maturation result.

This is genuine co-development, not a free technology grant.

A passive funder/host/recipient instead uses normal evidence/tacit/foreign-transfer assimilation and is not automatically Mature.

## Equal knowledge does not imply equal usability

Two participants can receive the same theory/data but have different practical outcomes.

Differences can come from:

- biology/biochemistry;
- facilities;
- materials;
- infrastructure;
- manufacturing tooling;
- expert/tacit knowledge;
- environmental compatibility.

The benchmark includes a joint foreign biological technology study where both parties reach Engineering Understood, but the compatible metabolic participant can reach Adapted Operation and Subsystem Replication while a synthetic incompatible participant remains Unusable with only Component Replication.

## Withdrawal

Withdrawal removes future real contribution when access actually ends.

Possible effects:

- project continues more slowly;
- project blocks because a unique facility/material/sample/expert disappeared;
- another participant replaces the contribution;
- project terminates while preserving completed science/history.

Withdrawal does not:

- roll completed progress backward;
- erase participants’ earned competence;
- recall already delivered records;
- physically retrieve hardware already transferred.

Diplomacy/law owns treaty breach, payment, trust, reputation, and political consequences.

## Hard facility loss can block

The benchmark uses `hazardous_foreign_tech_protocols`, whose Experimental stage canonically requires `xenoscience_containment`.

If the partner operating the only authorized containment facility withdraws it, remaining labs cannot substitute. The stage becomes concretely blocked until equivalent legitimate facility access is restored.

## Communications / distributed continuity

Joint programs use the existing distributed scientific continuity model.

There is no generic cross-polity coordination penalty.

If remote data is required and the communication link is down, that dependency can block while local work continues.

Existing local records remain local. Reconnection resumes pending data delivery rather than recreating already completed work.

## Classified collaboration

Joint projects may use Restricted/Classified/Special-Access security under milestone #11.

Participants receive only authorized compartments.

A designated integration context may have all engineering-critical compartments even while no participant independently receives the complete package.

Contributing to a project does not automatically grant missing compartments.

Leaks/espionage remain Intelligence/Security actions; Research interprets factual compromised records normally.

## Multispecies / biochemical collaboration

Biological collaboration remains applicability scoped.

A participant can contribute:

- comparative samples;
- biochemistry expertise;
- specialized containment;
- compatible biological facilities;

without implying its own population can use the resulting biology.

Cross-biochemistry results still obey the existing population-scoped compatibility/capability model.

## Fair AI

AI uses the same collaboration queries as the player-facing UI.

AI knows only:

- offered/known partner contributions;
- legitimately known partner capabilities;
- disclosed objective/result rights;
- real communication/security conditions.

It does not inspect hidden partner technologies, unoffered facilities, or exact unseen capacity.

Harder AI receives no free collaboration RP or hidden result rights.

## Runtime boundary

Canonical machine files:

- `research_collaboration_model.json`
- `research_collaboration_runtime_extension.json`
- `research_collaboration_benchmark_scenarios.json`

The runtime extension exposes seven factual input events and six queries.

## Persistence / performance

Persist detailed state only for active/strategically relevant scientific collaborations.

Use:

- aggregate contribution records;
- physical asset references rather than copied assets;
- bounded pending result deliveries;
- compressed completed collaboration history.

Never create:

- one technology graph per partner;
- per-scientist collaboration state;
- detailed research state for a diplomatic agreement that has no actual scientific work.

## Benchmarks

Milestone #12 tests:

1. real labs with one shared diminishing-return curve and no treaty multiplier;
2. withdrawal removing future capacity without reversing progress;
3. hard facility withdrawal blocking a stage;
4. asymmetric result usability despite equal records;
5. classified joint compartments with a designated integration context;
6. communication partition blocking only a real remote-data dependency;
7. 1,000-year bounded collaboration-state growth.

These are deterministic architecture/regression fixtures, not final commercial balance.
