# Adaptive Research Benchmark Notes

The initial benchmark baseline demonstrates that the current public research graph can produce meaningful divergent technological histories and bounded state under deterministic offline conditions.

It does **not** mean current RP costs, lab schedules, agenda weights, or Mature-node counts are final Early Access balance.

The strongest current signals are:

- same-origin research histories can diverge substantially without separate species trees;
- natural complacency can allow a challenger to leapfrog without any hidden catch-up multiplier;
- a former leader can recognize changed circumstances and increase attention without a guaranteed comeback;
- foreign technology can retain brokerage value when unusable to the holder;
- 1,000-year research records can remain comfortably bounded when static catalog/index data is shared and recent/history buffers are capped.

Future normal-public technology expansion should keep these scenarios passing unless an intentional, documented design change justifies changing a threshold.

The next catalog/content expansion should especially improve biochemical/species diversity while preserving the shared possibility-graph model: unusual-solvent carbon biology, silicon-centered speculative biology, cryogenic/hydrocarbon biospheres, and compatibility/adaptation research should be represented by traits/capabilities and alternative research families rather than separate race trees.
