# Research Agenda, Scientific Culture & AI Planning

Adaptive Research needs player agency and believable AI behavior without turning the hidden possibility graph into a shopping list.

The solution is a **Research Agenda**.

The player/government/AI can decide what problems, fields, domains, and known capabilities deserve attention. Those priorities shape background science, project recommendations, competence preservation, and requests for physical scientific capacity. They **do not directly unlock technologies or multiply Research Points**.

## Research Agenda

The agenda is deliberately high-level.

A civilization can prioritize recognized:

- research domains
- knowledge fields
- problems represented by known Research Pressure
- cross-lineage capabilities it knows it needs

It also maintains broad orientations such as:

- basic vs applied science
- competence preservation
- portfolio diversity
- foreign-science engagement

Priority levels are:

1. Deprioritized
2. Routine
3. Important
4. Strategic
5. Critical

These are planning/attention levels, not RP multipliers.

## What priority actually changes

Priority can change:

- where unassigned laboratories place background scientific attention;
- which currently visible projects are recommended most strongly;
- which fields receive training/competence-preservation attention;
- which additional labs/facilities/samples/training pipelines/foreign experts the research system asks other systems to obtain;
- which legitimately known foreign packages receive assimilation/evaluation attention.

Priority cannot:

- make an Unknown technology appear by decree;
- bypass prerequisites/evidence/biology/facilities;
- turn pressure directly into RP;
- create laboratories or money;
- reveal the hidden possibility graph.

The player can still directly start **visible Investigable** projects when all requirements are met. The distinction is that the player cannot order scientists to research a concept the civilization does not yet know exists.

## Research capacity requests

Research can tell the wider simulation what science wants.

Examples:

- more propulsion-specialized lab capacity;
- a high-energy research complex;
- a planetary environment simulation facility;
- a training pipeline to preserve materials-engineering practice;
- a xenoscience containment facility;
- a foreign expert cohort;
- a captured sample or manufacturing-process package.

These are **requests**, not spawned assets.

Construction/economy/government/diplomacy systems decide whether the civilization can actually provide them.

That creates real opportunity cost. A government can declare propulsion Strategic while refusing to fund the expensive facility propulsion researchers say they need.

## Scientific culture

Scientific culture is a small mutable civilization/institutional state rather than an immutable species bonus.

Current research-facing axes include:

- Scientific Curiosity
- Research Risk Tolerance
- Institutional Conservatism
- Threat Sensitivity
- Complacency Tendency
- Long-Term Orientation
- Scientific Openness
- Scientific Secrecy
- Reproducibility Rigor
- Scientific Prestige Competition
- Portfolio Diversity
- Commercialization Orientation

These axes affect **behavior**, not universal research speed.

They can change through government reform, leadership, wars, successful or catastrophic research programs, international scientific exchange, commercialization, censorship, institutional collapse, and generational change.

A species can begin with culturally justified biases, but those values are not permanent biological laws.

## Natural complacency and catch-up

A technological leader should not receive a hidden penalty for being ahead.

Instead, suppose a civilization's warships have decisively outperformed every opponent it has legitimately observed for a century.

Its scientists and military institutions may conclude that the current propulsion/weapons doctrine is adequate.

If the civilization also has:

- high complacency;
- high institutional conservatism;
- low threat sensitivity;
- few recent military failures;

then military research can gradually lose agenda priority and requests for new military research infrastructure can decline.

Those resources may instead move toward medicine, civilian industry, fundamental science, planetary engineering, culture, or other needs.

Nothing forces this.

A paranoid empire may keep military research at Strategic even while dominant. A high-curiosity technocracy may continue pushing the frontier simply because scientific advancement matters culturally. A commercially competitive civilization may innovate because civilian industry rewards it.

Meanwhile a weaker rival facing repeated losses gains real Research Pressure and legitimate evidence that better capabilities are possible. Its agenda may shift aggressively toward the problem.

If the leader later observes that rival closing the gap, its own threat perception and research agenda can rise again.

Therefore technological convergence can emerge without:

- `#1 technology = -25% research`;
- `behind = +50% catch-up`;
- hidden global rankings;
- free RP for weak civilizations.

The weaker civilization may catch up, fail to catch up, or leapfrog through a different solution.

## Fair-information AI research planning

AI research planning uses exactly the same materialized research information and authoritative blockers as player-facing systems.

It can use:

- its visible research horizon;
- its current pressures;
- its field competence;
- its laboratories/facilities/tacit expertise;
- its known capabilities and gaps;
- its scientific culture;
- high-level strategic goals;
- legitimately observed foreign capabilities;
- known technology packages/market offers;
- known resource/economic constraints.

It cannot use:

- Unknown graph nodes;
- exact unseen enemy technologies;
- exact unseen enemy research projects;
- a hidden universal technology ranking;
- secret future triggers;
- omniscient technology-market demand.

## AI planning layers

### 1. Agenda review

Triggered by meaningful events or low-frequency strategic review.

It decides which recognized fields/problems/capabilities deserve attention and what scientific capacity the civilization should seek.

### 2. Project shortlist

When a directed research slot/capacity becomes available, the AI builds a bounded shortlist from **visible Investigable projects only**.

The current seed maximum is 12 candidates.

### 3. Project selection

The AI chooses among the shortlist and submits the same start/pause/resume/reallocate commands available through the player UI.

The authoritative research runtime revalidates the request.

### 4. Capacity planning

The AI can request labs, facilities, training, samples, and foreign scientific access. External systems decide whether those requests are affordable or politically possible.

## Candidate utility

AI project utility considers contextual components such as:

- recognized need;
- strategic alignment;
- capability gap value;
- Project Readiness;
- time to useful effect;
- lab opportunity cost;
- whether another solution already covers the need;
- portfolio diversity;
- hypothesis/hazard uncertainty;
- long-term scientific value;
- foreign acquisition alternatives;
- knowledge spillover.

There is no single permanent global weight vector.

Weights vary with agenda, scientific culture, government strategy, and current circumstances. They rank **visible choices**; they do not change the physics of research.

AI decisions should retain an explanation such as:

> High priority because repeated missile losses created a severe defensive need and Point Defense is currently feasible.

or:

> Propulsion research deprioritized because all observed opponents remain substantially slower and current fleet mobility is assessed as adequate.

or:

> High-energy project deferred because the civilization lacks the required experimental complex.

This will be valuable for both debugging and player-facing AI behavior explanations.

## Avoiding project thrashing

AI should not switch projects every time a score moves slightly.

Project continuity considers:

- existing RP progress;
- specialist/facility setup;
- strategic continuity;
- institutional conservatism.

Meaningful switching triggers include major new threats, a hard blocker, a hypothesis result, strategic-goal change, or a dramatically better visible option.

This is institutional inertia, not a hidden sunk-cost research bonus.

## Foreign technology decisions

AI can compare native research with a known foreign route.

For example:

> Develop native point defense in 11 estimated years

versus:

> Buy a compatible foreign package now but remain dependent on imported manufacturing tooling.

It can also recognize legitimate brokerage value if it knows another civilization wants a technology it cannot use itself.

It never receives omniscient market knowledge.

## Difficulty

Harder AI can improve:

- planning horizon;
- candidate comparison;
- capacity coordination;
- reaction quality;
- memory use;
- avoidance of self-defeating research thrashing.

Harder AI does **not** receive:

- hidden-tree access;
- exact enemy technology;
- free RP;
- free laboratories;
- free evidence;
- prerequisite bypasses.

Any future explicit scenario handicap should be visible as a scenario rule rather than secretly embedded in Adaptive Research.

## Performance

Research agenda/AI planning is not a per-tick system.

It is event-driven with bounded low-frequency reviews.

The AI:

- ranks visible candidates only;
- shortlists a bounded number;
- caches utility until material inputs change;
- uses coarser review intervals for dormant civilizations;
- never compares every civilization's entire research state against every other civilization every tick.
